# Game experience cue audit — 1 November 2026

Source and Studio startup audit, not a claim that every effect was heard, seen or enjoyed by a new player. Reviewed client/server/lobby gameplay modules, item and wardrobe visual recipes, current exhibit audio configuration and the new-player report. Priority is based on how much a cue helps a player act correctly, not spectacle.

## Direct assessment

**Quick-start update, 1 November:** The original 4/10 assessment below was made before the one-tap tutorial depot ride. Studio Play now confirms that, at the assigned museum, the first-step guide shows a visible RIDE TO DEPOT button and pickup marker; the ride actually moves the player to the depot. The guide also offers STEAL AGAIN for a short time after the first earned dollar. A startup timing defect was found and fixed: the guide now finds the pickup marker even when the runtime target is created after the initial lookup. The return trip, protected delivery and $1 income wait still have to be played. A **provisional 6/10 experience estimate** is more honest than claiming 8/10 from a solo Studio smoke test. The target is 8/10 after three unfamiliar-player sessions show a satisfying first payoff within 90 seconds and few navigation/help failures; no such sessions were run here. Phone and controller presentation still need direct observation.

**First-90-second hook confidence: 4/10, provisional.** There is a clear heist premise, working first item, protected delivery and visual guide. The remaining risk is the length and cognitive load of the first trip: the tutorial asks the player to travel to the depot, hold pickup, cross a long route back, find the receiving area, hold delivery, wait for the first $1 at $6/min, then understand Collection and public stock. Historical open routes are roughly 300–327 studs and covered routes 349–410; a new player can spend much of 90 seconds simply travelling. The guide and new world destination sign help orientation but do not prove excitement. No unfamiliar seven-year-old or first-90-second timed test was run this turn. This score is an evidence-limited product judgment, not a measured retention metric.

The most useful improvement is **one clear response to each successful action**: “I took it,” “I got away,” “it is now on my stand,” and “I am earning.” Add small effects and sounds at those moments before adding ambience everywhere. Avoid letting decorative item sounds, Warden warnings and music compete.

More per-item noise is not automatically better.
- `src/server/Services/TravelEffects.luau` already has warp sound, sparks and rings. `MuseumDoors.luau` has opening sound and motion. `src/shared/WardrobeAppearance.luau` has a quiet Phantom Glow sound and visual effects. `CollectionHud.luau` pulses stand glow. `NotificationHud.luau` animates messages. The lobby wardrobe now has signage, mannequin/mirror and an outline highlight.
- `src/client/TutorialGuideController.luau` has a compass arrow and now a short world sign at pickup **and** delivery. Guard warning is represented by the existing `GuardHud.luau`; Task 14B sound/cinematic work remains deferred.
- The profile already stores `masterVolume`, `musicVolume`, `effectsVolume`, `reduceMotion` and `lowEffects`. Source inspection found no general background-music player. No music asset or license has been chosen. `src/client/OperationsMenu.luau` and the profile settings path should be checked when adding a soundtrack so the stored music slider actually controls it.

**Background music:** Add only after a playable, properly licensed/owned loop is selected. Use a calm instrumental heist bed around the museum/lobby, with no dominant melody or sharp transients. Start at a low mix level, route through the saved music setting, fade during guard warning/capture and important delivery cues, and test through phone speakers and headphones. Make music optional; keep gameplay information available without it. Prefer one or two well-mixed loops over several simultaneous tracks. Do not use an arbitrary public audio ID or introduce music into the existing short exhibit SFX config.

## Order and acceptance

1. Time the first 90 seconds with at least three unfamiliar players, including a child in the target age range and a phone user. Record time to find pickup, pickup completion, first return, delivery, first dollar, first public-stock choice, and any help request. Ask what they think storage, display and income mean. A new player should be able to reach the first satisfying payoff within 90 seconds on an ordinary route; if not, fix route/onboarding friction before adding effects.
2. Implement the P1 *action confirmations* with server outcome events as truth. Add a tiny audio/VFX budget and cooldowns so repeated 6-player events do not overwhelm the mix or phone GPU. Test normal, low-effects, reduced-motion and muted settings. Maintain clear guard and delivery cues at all mix settings.
3. Add low background music after obtaining a usable track, then level-match against existing exhibit sounds, door, warp, guard and pickup/delivery cues. Measure whether a player can still hear the warning and recognize a completed delivery.
4. Only then consider P2/P3 ambience and Task 14B cinematic polish. Do not compensate for confusing route or inventory feedback with louder effects.
